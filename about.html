How I got here

I started at Las Positas College and transferred into UC San Diego's Mathematics–Computer Science program with an A.S. in Mathematics and Computer Science, earning Provost Honors in Spring 2025. I'm minoring in Cognitive Science alongside the CS coursework, a combination that's shaped how I think about model behavior less as pure statistics and more as a system that has to interact reasonably with people and messy, real-world data.

My coursework has run through the standard ML/AI sequence: Advanced Machine Learning Methods, Supervised Machine Learning, Artificial Intelligence, Statistical NLP, Data Analysis and Inference, and Probability, alongside project work in reinforcement learning (MDPs, Q-learning, MCTS), computer vision (epipolar geometry, RANSAC, camera models), and numerical linear algebra (SVD, QR, condition numbers).

What I actually care about

Every project on this site started from some version of the same question: what does this model get wrong, and does it know it? A win-probability model that's 68% accurate on average but badly overconfident during back-to-back games isn't done: it's half-finished. A price prediction that hits its coverage target overall but fails on expensive vehicles specifically hasn't earned trust yet. An LLM agent that produces a fluent itinerary with a hallucinated destination hasn't actually solved the task.

So I gravitate toward the parts of ML that are about honesty under uncertainty: calibration, conformal prediction, walk-forward backtesting, guardrails on generative output, rather than just squeezing out the last point of accuracy. I'd rather ship something that tells you where it's shaky than something that just looks good in a demo.

How I work

Every project here shipped as a tested, deployed artifact, not a notebook. That means automated test suites (40 to 312 tests depending on the project), CI via GitHub Actions, and either a live demo or a reproducible pipeline someone else could actually run. I'd rather spend the extra time on that than on a polished write-up of something that only ever ran once on my laptop.
